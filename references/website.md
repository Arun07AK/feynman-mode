# The website

A polished, visual coordinator for the whole subject. A frictionless experience matters more than clever engineering.

## Design freedom

You have complete freedom over the interface and its interactions. Build what suits this subject best. The principles below are what the design must satisfy, not a layout to copy.

The first harness built this way used one board showing the whole course in study order, with modules as regions and topics as cards. A card opened its topic in a side panel, and the thread button came first. Read that as an example of the principles, then design freely.

## Principles

- **A study page shows only what the learner acts on.** Sources, evidence, audit notes and "why chosen" go in the docs, not on the page.
- Keep cognitive load, navigation and repeated actions to a minimum. Priorities, prerequisites, progress, past questions and revision are all available without crowding the screen.
- The thread button is the first action on every topic.
- No lesson text anywhere on the site.
- A plain index the learner follows themselves: no planner, no recommender, no "today's plan".
- Every count and total comes from the data files.
- Progress is saved locally and survives a reload.
- Ask the learner once about device and theme. If they have no preference, build for desktop with a light theme.
- Take the exam date from the learner or their calendar. If you do not have it, say so. Do not assume one.

## What the site holds

**The index** is the page the learner returns to constantly. It shows the curriculum's structure, progress, priorities, what to learn next, saved threads, mapped lectures and what needs revision.

**A topic view** holds: title, stars, prerequisites, objectives, a short orientation (what the thread will walk through), the thread button, the saved link and done status, the lecture or its documented absence, and the topic's past questions.

**A roadmap** lists every topic in study order with its stars, prerequisites, progress, thread button and lecture (course, section, times, length, link, cost). Consecutive lectures are grouped into runs, with watched ticks, progress per run and overall, viewing time totalled from the data, and a short "What you need" list of the courses and their costs.

**Practice, mocks and the revision layer**, as described in `practice.md`.

## The flow it serves

Open the index → learn the topic in the thread → use the lecture for another view, before, during or after → practise → mark it done → next topic.
