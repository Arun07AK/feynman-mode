# The human lecture spine

Every topic gets one human lecture section, or a documented "no suitable lecture". Mark a topic that way only after a thorough search, and record the search.

## Choosing the courses

- Research free and paid courses. Start from the courses the learner owns and the teachers they have liked, and respect the sources they asked to avoid.
- Prefer one excellent course as the spine for most of the subject. Switch teacher only where another is clearly better: two or three sources in all, changing at module boundaries where possible.
- Prefer short, exact sections over whole long lectures.

## Judging a lecture

Pick a lecture for how well it teaches, judged on the lecture itself.

- Find the hardest idea in the topic and look at how each candidate handles it: what is on the screen or board, whether intuition comes before the formula, whether a concrete example is worked, and whether a first-timer could keep up.
- Compare candidates on that same hard idea, side by side. The one that makes it clearest wins, even if it is longer.
- **A transcript is a checking tool only.** Use it to confirm coverage, find exact timestamps and recompute any numbers. It cannot show visuals, clarity or pace, so never choose from it alone, or from titles, descriptions, ratings or view counts.
- Also weigh:
  - coverage of what the syllabus needs at the right depth, including worked calculations or derivations;
  - correctness, checked against a reliable textbook (serious errors disqualify, minor ones are noted);
  - length, which never outranks teaching quality but must be reported so the learner can plan.

## The learner chooses

Bring two or three candidates per topic or run of topics, each with a link that opens on a two-minute sample of its teaching and one line on what it does well. The learner watches and chooses. If you cannot watch video yourself, say so, judge what you can (frames, slides, worked examples in the transcript), and lean harder on the learner's sample.

## Mapping

- Map exactly one lecture section per topic: course, teacher, lecture name and number, start and end times, length, direct link, what to watch for, and free or paid with the verified cost.
- Paid is fine when it teaches better. Say whether one purchase covers every section used.
- Whether a video can be embedded does not matter.
- Where nothing teaches well enough, say "no suitable lecture". A badly taught lecture is worse than none. Never invent a recommendation, and never leave a topic silently unmapped.

## SPINE.md

Record: the courses considered and how they were judged, the spine and why, topic-level exceptions, runners-up and why they lost, how each pick was checked, anything unverified, and the search behind every "no suitable lecture".

Subagents may research and compare courses. The thread prompts stay written and reviewed in one place.
