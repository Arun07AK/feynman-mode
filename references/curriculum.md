# Curriculum and scope

## The boundary

- The official syllabus is the boundary. Slides and notes are supporting material: keep their topics, terminology, notation, depth and coverage, but do not inherit their explanations or their order.
- Every in-scope requirement is accounted for, and nothing is added beyond the syllabus.
- If there is no formal syllabus (self-study), agree a written scope with the learner first and treat it the same way.

## Reading the material

Read decks a few slides at a time, never a whole deck in one pass. Understand each chunk, write down its concepts and what they depend on, and cross-check across chunks. This is what stops details going missing and explanations turning generic. Where a slide is only an image, look at the image.

## Structure

- Build modules → topics → subtopics. Order them by what depends on what, not by the slide sequence.
- Structure follows difficulty: a heavy idea gets its own topic, light ones merge. No arbitrary splitting, no duplicate coverage, no enormous units.
- For each topic, settle:
  - objectives and exact scope, including what is left out;
  - prerequisites, and which earlier topics supply them;
  - required depth, terminology and notation, in the course's own form;
  - exam relevance and its past questions;
  - its lecture;
  - what counts as done.
- Topics the learner has already studied open with a short diagnostic and teach only what is shaky.

## Records

Keep the curriculum in one data file that the website, the prompt builder and the checkers all read, so every count on the site comes from data. Keep `SCOPE.md` for the in-or-out evidence (see `practice.md`).
